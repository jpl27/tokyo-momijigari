Tokyo grew from about one million people in Edo times to over fourteen million today. But "Tokyo" has meant four different areas. Each line below measures one of them.

Tokyo's population, and its share of Japan

    Left scale: millions of people. Right scale: share of Japan's population, in percent. Tap or hover on the chart for exact numbers and sources. Arrow keys also work. Every number is also in the table below.

    Why four lines? The name "Tokyo" has covered four different areas. Comparing the lines shows how much depends on where the border is drawn.

    Edo numbers are scholars' estimates. Officials counted only the townspeople, about 500,000 to 575,000. Samurai households were never counted, so the shaded band shows the range of guesses, 1.0 to 1.3 million (sources 9, 11 and 12).

    Tokyo City jumps in 1932, when it took in the towns around it and grew from 15 to 35 wards. Those 35 wards are almost exactly today's 23-ward area.

    Tokyo Prefecture also takes in the Tama area and the islands. It jumps in 1894, when three Tama districts moved over from Kanagawa.

    After 1868 many samurai went home, and the city shrank. It took until the 1890s to regain Edo's peak size (Smith, 1978).

    A warning about two scales. The pink share line uses the right-hand scale. Where it seems to cross a population line, nothing real happens there. The crossing is only a side effect of how the two scales are drawn.
